Shared MHD batch kernels¶

The native two_component_energy_batch, three_component_energy_batch and perpendicular_kinetic_energy_batch interfaces evaluate the same definitions as their scalar counterparts. The generated ideal_mhd_batch module also provides the KS3 frozen-in Hessian contraction. All variants come from gen/ideal_mhd/gen_ideal_mhd_batches.f90 and ideal_mhd_symbolic.f90.

A call is call NAME(n, inputs, outputs), with contiguous structure-of-arrays storage inputs(n,2*m+1) and outputs(n,m+2). Columns contain the m physical inputs, their m tangents, and one cotangent; outputs are value, JVP, and the m VJP entries. FP64 input/output is the reference. FP32 inputs may have FP32 or FP64 outputs; the latter boundary preserves FP32 arithmetic and permits FP64 caller reductions. It does not restore precision lost inside the leaf.

The coordinate domain requires J nonzero, B and grad_s2 positive, and nonzero flux norm; mass requires positive density. The signed-J branches are tested separately; derivatives cannot cross J=0. No new enclosure claim is made. The xi form is the gamma=0 plasma-volume Hessian at the identity. It supplies no vacuum, equilibrium-boundary or complete global spectral operator.

CPU loops use OpenMP SIMD and static thread scheduling without allocation. The CUDA header has equivalent mhd_FAMILY_PRECISION_batch(n,x,y) global entry points and zero-based packed SoA storage. The caller owns streams, launch geometry, residency, reductions and precision admission. Ordinary GLISS builds do not require a CUDA compiler or CAS.

The standalone native oracle and timing harness is in benchmarks/ideal_mhd_batches. Configure normally for CPU or add -DMHD_BATCH_CUDA=ON -DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=120 for the RTX 5060 Ti. ctest runs analytic slab/mass limits, directional differences, adjoint pairings and precision checks. The CUDA oracle compares every output against the CPU reference and checks tail sentinels. Timing arguments are family (0=all, 1=two, 2=three, 3=mass, 4=xi), batch size and repetitions; the CUDA executable takes batch size and repetitions. Each uses two warmups.

[V] GNU 12.2 cluster measurements at 262144 points, 11 samples: FP32/FP64 speedups at 1/8/24 threads are two 1.98/2.21/3.10, three 2.03/1.91/2.69, mass 1.42/1.54/1.59, xi 2.13/2.13/2.22. FP64 24-thread scaling is 10.74/11.72/14.03/17.29 respectively. These shared-host kernel results include value/JVP/VJP output, with FP64 reductions outside the timed kernel. They establish neither a solver speedup nor general FP32 admission.

The caller’s accepted residuals and certificates remain FP64. The new entry points do not switch solver precision automatically. A candidate stage must be admitted against its own sensitivity/error budget, with escalation and an independent final residual. BF16/FP16 are storage formats supplied by the shared precision runtime, not native arithmetic kinds in these leaves.
