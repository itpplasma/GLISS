Free-boundary model¶

StabilityProblem(vacuum=VacuumModel(...)) retains the plasma-edge normal displacement and adds the magnetic energy of the current-free vacuum field it drives. A scalar-potential boundary-integral solve supplies that vacuum energy, with either decay at infinity or an ideal conducting wall. VacuumModel sets a full-torus edge mesh and an optional conformal or explicit Cartesian wall enclosing the plasma. The reported energy decomposition includes the vacuum contribution.

The equilibrium model assumes:

  • zero plasma pressure at the edge;

  • continuity of the tangential equilibrium magnetic field across the edge, matching a current-free exterior;

  • no equilibrium surface-current sheet requiring an additional surface term.

Callers must qualify these conditions for the imported equilibrium. The constructor checks input shapes, geometry and numerical admission; it does not solve the equilibrium exterior matching problem or certify these physical assumptions. An eigenpair certificate concerns the assembled matrix pencil. It does not certify equilibrium force balance, edge matching, or convergence of the volume and vacuum discretizations.

GVEC exports may supply explicit interface data in addition to the ordinary interior half-grid. Native ingestion accepts numeric boundary_schema_version=1, scalar boundary_s=1 with units 1, and boundary_dPhi_ds and boundary_dchi_ds with units Wb. The six boundary_{xhat,yhat,zhat}_{mnc,mns} arrays use the existing (m,n) Fourier grid and units m; stellarator symmetry permits the unused sine/cosine complements to be omitted. Positions use the declared Boozer position frame. The vacuum operator uses these positions and independently evaluated flux derivatives directly. Interior geometry and profile splines retain their half-grid contract. Malformed or unsupported declarations fail ingestion.

Without an explicit boundary declaration, positions retain the legacy extrapolation to s=1, whose accuracy requires separate qualification. Flux profiles are never silently extrapolated: a half-grid input requires independent edge slopes via VacuumModel(edge_flux_slopes=(dPhi_ds, dchi_ds)) (Wb), or plasma_vacuum_model_t%edge_flux_slopes in Fortran. The slopes are recorded in configuration documents and hashes. A profile grid containing s=1 can instead supply its endpoint derivative. Schema 1 owns its declared slopes and ignores this schema-0 override. Schema 1 supplies no edge pressure or matched exterior field, so its presence alone does not certify the physical assumptions above.

The vacuum boundary mesh and volume angular quadrature are independent. Use admitted volume angular grids, then refine the radial mesh, displacement mode range and vacuum edge mesh separately. Record the wall geometry, equilibrium and solver identities for a comparison. Parity-separated spectra require symmetry of the complete plasma-vacuum operator, including the wall. A wall that breaks that symmetry requires the coupled parity operator so its cross-parity stiffness is retained.

The native toroidal-harmonic oracle checks absolute vacuum energies with and without a wall. The public Solov’ev benchmark compares a critical conformal wall distance with DCON and studies discretization convergence. The October 2 export qualification found that an ordinary GVEC half-grid export does not meet the frozen native edge flux-slope and rotational-transform limits. The complete quantitative plasma-vacuum acceptance case therefore remains open; the vacuum oracle alone does not qualify that imported equilibrium. Historical benchmark results obtained before the current angular admission policy require a fresh run; qualitative stabilization and energy closure alone do not establish quantitative agreement of the complete plasma-vacuum problem.
